# Ellipsis and Substitution in IT - Grammar Guide

## Ellipsis

### Omitting Words

**Definition:** Omitting words when meaning is clear without them.

> "The server was down yesterday, but **is working** today." (is = is working)

### Uses in IT

> "I checked the logs." — "Did you check **them**?" (ellipsis)
> "The system crashed, but **has been restored**." (has been restored = has been restored)

---

## Substitution

### Replacing Words

**Definition:** Replacing repeated elements with words like "one," "so," "do."

| Substitutor | Use | Example |
|-------------|-----|----------|
| one | replace noun | "Use the new software, or stick with the old **one**." |
| so | replace clause | "I think so." |
| do/does/did | replace verb | "Did you test it?" — "Yes, I **did**." |
| that | replace clause | "I believe **that** is correct." |

### Uses in IT

> "Use the new server, or we could use the old **one**."
> "I tested the system, and she **did** too."

---

## IT Examples

### Ellipsis
> "The server was down, but **is now up**."
> "Did you complete the task?" — "**Yes, I have**."

### Substitution
> "Use the new application, or use the old **one**."
> "I think **so**." / "I don't think **so**."
> "Did you check?" — "Yes, I **did**."
