# Adjectives Ending in -ing and -ed: Boring/Bored - Grammar Guide

## Introduction

The difference is between what causes the feeling vs. the feeling itself.

---

## -ing Adjectives

### Use: Cause of Feeling (Descriptive)

| adjective | Meaning |
|-----------|---------|
| boring | causes boredom |
| interesting | causes interest |
| exciting | causes excitement |
| challenging | causes challenge |

> "The **boring** task took all day."
> "The presentation was **interesting**."

---

## -ed Adjectives

### Use: Personal Feeling

| adjective | Meaning |
|-----------|---------|
| bored | feeling bored |
| interested | feeling interested |
| excited | feeling excited |
| overwhelmed | feeling overwhelmed |

> "I am **bored** with this work."
> "The team is **excited** about the project."

---

## Key Differences

| -ing (Cause) | -ed (Feeling) |
|-------------|--------------|
| "The task is boring" | "I am bored" |
| "The meeting is interesting" | "She is interested" |
| Describes the thing | Describes the person |

---

## Uses in IT

### -ing
> "The debugging process is **time-consuming**."
> "The new feature is quite **challenging**."
> "The training is **enriching**."

### -ed
> "I am **bored** with repetitive tasks."
> "The team is **excited** about the new project."
> "She feels **overwhelmed** with the workload."

## Practice Exercises

### Choose the correct form:

1. The work is ___. (causing boredom) → **boring**
2. I am ___. (feeling bored) → **bored**
3. The task is ___. (causes challenge) → **challenging**
4. She is ___. (feeling excited) → **excited**
5. The project is ___. (excites) → **exciting**

---

## Common Mistakes

### ❌ "I'm boring" vs "I'm bored"
> "I'm boring" = I cause boredom to others
> "I'm bored" = I feel bored

---

## Quick Summary

| Ending | Focus |
|--------|-------|
| **-ing** | The cause (the thing/task) |
| **-ed** | The feeling (the person) |
